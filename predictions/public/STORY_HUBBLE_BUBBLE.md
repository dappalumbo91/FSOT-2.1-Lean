Imagine you and a friend measure the “speed of the wind” — but you’re standing in different rooms of the same house. One room has a fan. One has a sealed door. One opens to a corridor where air is bleeding from a broken window.

You both use honest instruments. You both get different numbers.  
The house is not broken. **You’re not sampling the same flow.**

## The usual story

Planck (and friends) look at the early universe through the cosmic microwave background and prefer an expansion rate near **67**.

SH0ES (and friends) climb a ladder of Cepheid stars in nearby galaxies and prefer something near **73**.

The fight is usually cast as: *who is wrong?* or *what new particle do we invent?*

## The FSOT story

Spacetime is a **fluid**. Black holes and white holes are not only endpoints of collapse — they participate in a **bleed** of structure and information through that fluid. Expanding nebulae, sightlines, and how a measurement couples to the medium change the **local density sector** you read.

So:

- A CMB acoustic ruler is not sitting in the same sector as a Cepheid in a star-forming disk on a particular sky sightline.  
- A TRGB measurement in a halo is not the same sector again.  
- **One fluid. Many legitimate readouts.**

FSOT freezes a prediction for **each tool** and, where we can, **each host galaxy**:

```text
H0_tool = H0_global × (1 + density_model × bleed)
```

Global background from the seed engine is about **68.44**.  
Bleed is small (~1.5%) but enough, across density sectors from depleted to inflated, to span roughly **67 → 75** — the same range the literature already occupies.

We are not asking the universe to pick a favorite press conference.  
We are asking whether **sector structure** is the missing grammar of the tension.
