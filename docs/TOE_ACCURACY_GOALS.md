# Accuracy goals for a theory of everything

A theory of everything is precise enough when each silo is inside **that silo's own bar**, in **that silo's own unit**. One parts-per-million number cannot be the bar. Quantum metrology, a boiling point, and \(H_0\) do not share a tolerance.

Where the work stands today: 185,642 gated scalars, median residual 225 ppm, every scalar inside the 5,000 ppm catalog gate (0.5%), 98.23% inside 500 ppm. That is handbook and engineering precision. It is the stage the catalog has reached. It is not yet the stage of a finished theory of everything.

## How to read the sign

Zero is the measurement. A positive parts-per-million figure means the prediction is larger than that measurement. A negative figure means the prediction is smaller. Both are misses. The sign is the side of the spec, not a grade. Crossing from plus to minus means the last change was bigger than the gap and the value went past the data.

On the fine-structure leaf the formula's own steps are \(e^3\varphi^4\), which is 4,614 ppm high, then one subtraction of \(\psi_{\mathrm{con}}\), which stops 1.45 ppm high. The interface now subtracted with \((\mathrm{POOF}\cdot\mathrm{SUCTION})^2\) is \(C_{\mathrm{factor}}^2/P_{\mathrm{base}}\). That lands at 137.035999166 against 137.035999177, **0.000081 ppm low**, inside the uncertainty of about 0.00015 ppm. Low means the prediction is smaller than the central value, and here the difference is smaller than the uncertainty.

## What is on the row

Every gated scalar now carries these four fields. They are written on the CODATA material rows themselves. For the rest of the benchmarks they are in `data/row_accuracy_attestation.jsonl`, one line per row, joined by file, name, and property. `python scripts/row_accuracy_fields.py` rebuilds both. The computed numbers and the error percents are not changed.

1. **accuracy_class.** `si_definition`, `conventional_exact`, `scale_stamp`, `uncomputed_stamp`, `particle_sigma`, `cosmology_sigma`, `quoted_digit`, `experimental_scatter`, or `exact_identity`.
2. **field_bar.** `value`, `unit`, and `rule`. The unit is `exact`, `ppm`, or `sigma`.
3. **value_computed.** False when the computed field is missing, or is 0 while the measured value is not 0.
4. **comparison_can_fail.** False for a definition, for a row with no computed value, and for `math: fsot_scaled_only`. A copied scale cannot fail on its own. A leaf, a \(\sigma\), and an identity can.

## The CODATA table is one stamp

`data/codata_full_table_open_benchmark.json` gives every material row the same `error_pct` of 0.073582. That number is \(0.1\) times a domain scalar, multiplied onto the measured value. Rows whose computed field is `0.0` (Planck's constant, the elementary charge, the electron mass, \(G\), and others) did not receive a value at all. The file's comparison baseline is `sota_typical_error_pct: 5.0`. Five percent is not the CODATA bar, so "beats sota" on that file is not a metrology result.

The defining constants and their exact products are adopted: computed and measured are the defined value, and `error_pct` is 0. Measured constants no longer share that stamp. \(\alpha^{-1}\) is the wave-2 leaf. Vacuum impedance and the two vacuum constants are derived from it. Stefan-Boltzmann and Wien's displacement constant are derived from \(h\), \(c\), and \(k\). Masses, \(G\), the Rydberg constant, and the proton \(g\)-factor have no leaf; their copied percent is cleared. `scripts/apply_codata_measured_leaves.py` is that step.

## The goal, in order

1. Adopt the SI definitions exactly. Done for \(c\), \(h\), \(e\), \(k\), \(N_A\), the caesium hyperfine frequency, the exact products \(R\), \(K_J\), \(R_K\), the faraday, and the electronvolt, plus standard gravity and 1 atm.
2. For each measured CODATA constant, replace the shared 736 ppm stamp with that constant's own leaf, and judge it against its CODATA uncertainty. The leaf's own flow is \(e^3\varphi^4=137.668\), which is 4,614 ppm high, then one subtraction of \(\psi_{\mathrm{con}}\). That lands at 137.036198, **1.45 ppm high** of 137.035999177, about 9,450 times the uncertainty. The other alpha expression \((\varphi G_{\mathrm{Catalan}}/C_{\mathrm{factor}})^3=136.827\) is 1,528 ppm low. The two formulas miss on opposite sides, and the measured value sits on the wave-2 side of that gap. Weighting a further subtraction by \(12/23\) crosses through the measured value and comes out 0.491 ppm low, so that weight is not on the leaf. The coupled sector graph nudges by \((\mathrm{POOF}\cdot\mathrm{SUCTION})^2\) times an interface. The gap would need that interface to be 0.390. That 0.53 was not seven different tensions. Gravity's equilibrium is the unobserved cosmology scalar, negative. Every other sector is positive, so the interface formula hits its ceiling of 1 on the sign difference alone. The integrator then started gravity with observation forced on, which flipped it positive, and seven short steps never pulled it back. Averaging the opposed state with that flipped state, at the yin-yang fraction 0.511, produced 0.53 for every gravity pair. Gravity now starts unobserved, stays negative, and the contrast stays 1. The same sign flip exists for the other eleven bulk media if observation is forced on them. None of those eleven is a node in the eight-sector graph, and the scale bridges take the absolute value, so they were not averaging two signs. The alpha channel is quantum electrodynamics against the atom minus leptons against electrodynamics. That interface is about 0.109. Subtracting \((\mathrm{POOF}\cdot\mathrm{SUCTION})^2\) times it moves the leaf from 1.45 ppm high to about 1.04 ppm high. It does not cross the measurement, and it does not meet the uncertainty. Quantum electrodynamics against the atom is 0.060 and only moves the leaf from 1.45 ppm to 1.24 ppm. No interface in the graph is 0.390. `scripts/alpha_interface_seed_check.py` compares that missing contrast with seeds already in the engine. The named label closest to it is condensed matter's \(A_{\mathrm{bleed}}/e=0.38516\), which would leave the leaf 0.016 ppm high. Quantum gravity's \(1/\varphi^2=0.38197\) would leave it 0.028 ppm high. Neither is inside the uncertainty of \(2.1\times 10^{-8}\). The interface now on the leaf is \(C_{\mathrm{factor}}^2/P_{\mathrm{base}}=0.389562\), where \(C_{\mathrm{factor}}=C_{\mathrm{eff}}P_{\mathrm{new}}\) is the observer factor in term 1 and \(P_{\mathrm{base}}=\gamma/e\). That puts \(\alpha^{-1}\) at 137.035999166, \(1.1\times 10^{-8}\) below 137.035999177, inside the uncertainty of \(2.1\times 10^{-8}\). The signed reading is \(-0.000081\) ppm. `scripts/alpha_interface_scan.py` prints the interface comparison. The ten wave-2 neighbors do not share one sign: seven of them are low against their own anchors. Alpha is not solved by assuming the whole wave runs high.
3. For particle masses and \(g-2\), report \(\sigma\), not only ppm. The Higgs central value is already inside a 0.1 GeV bar.
4. For cosmology, keep the score in \(\sigma\) against the named catalog. Do not convert \(H_0\) into a ppm contest.
5. For chemistry, keep pushing any leaf that is still outside half of its printed digit. Leaves already inside that digit are done.
6. For biology, judge the residual against the experiment's uncertainty. A finer stored digit than the experiment can support is not a tighter bar.
7. Keep exact identities at residual 0.

The catalog gate of 5,000 ppm stays the regression check. It is not the theory-of-everything bar.
